# Why AI Agents Orchestrators Are In High Demand

CNBC International video (~11:45) on [[Wiki/Concepts/AI Orchestration|AI orchestration]] as a job, a skill and a capability.

## Summary
- **Definition**: AI orchestration is deciding how work is divided between humans and [[Wiki/Concepts/AI Agents|AI agents]] to complete whole business processes — "understanding a business and then defining what you don't want the machine to do and what you do want the machine to do."
- **Demand**: [[Wiki/Entities/Randstad|Randstad]]'s 2026 tech talent report puts orchestration/governance postings up **15× in four years**, ~400% year-over-year; [[Wiki/Entities/Draup|Draup]] reports **+1,700%** "agent orchestration" postings in banking for 2026.
- **Supply gap**: agentic AI skill postings up ~**1,300%** against only ~**300%** growth in supply.
- **What the role does**: design, build, connect and monitor multiple agents; handle handoffs between specialized agents; measure value; own testing and guardrails. "Think of it as an air traffic controller."
- **Who gets hired**: software engineering, systems integration, customer-facing work, process thinking, commercial judgment — plus MCP/agent-to-agent/agent-to-UI frameworks (MCP, LangGraph, Semantic Kernel).
- **Soft skills dominate**: 61% of customers find soft skills and critical thinking harder to source than technical skill; technical gets you in the door, thinking and communication build the career.
- **Pay**: US agentic AI roles average **$115k–$239k**; companies pay up to **79% more** for AI talent (nearly double in the UK and India).
- **Workforce**: not a simple replacement story — "for every agent, you might need a workforce that actually manages those agents"; one orchestrator becomes a team, then a business unit.
- **Sentiment risk**: Glassdoor negative mentions of AI in reviews rose from 15% (2019) to **53% (2026)**; leaders warning of replacement discourage experimentation.

## Key Claims
- "Is that a job or is it a skill or is it a capability? And it's really, it's all three."
- "We used to live in a world where it was a human-to-human workforce and now we have this human-to-human-to-agent workforce."
- "The air controller doesn't necessarily need to know how to fly a plane, but they need to make sure that tens of thousands of flights ... do not collide."
- "The challenge is AI is not always predictable. We have to test and test and test and make sure that everything we're building towards has the right guardrails."
- "When an AI agent speaks, your company speaks" — hence orchestrators belong in a senior role category.
- "AI orchestration, in my opinion, is a part of everybody's job in an organization."
- "For every agent, you might need a workforce that actually manages those agents. One AI orchestrator becomes three, becomes a team and an entire business unit."

## Raw Excerpts
### The evolution to agents (2:11)
> We started with these amazing large language models and we could interact with them by sending text. Then we needed to formulate that text, so we had prompt engineers. And then the next evolution is — why is it a human interacting? Why don't we put a piece of software around this and allow the software to control that interaction and then scale the value? That's where agents essentially came in.

### Handoff pattern (5:18)
> We have an energy general assistant. When particular scenarios come up where you need a specialized agent to take over — identifying that need, capturing the relevant information, and then handing off to a secondary agent. That agent will continue the path until it's resolved, then hand back to the primary agent. We can build that, but we have to monitor it.

### Sourcing and governance (8:43)
> Companies likely to win that race will bet on three types of sourcing: they can buy it, they can build it through training, and they can borrow it through flexible work models. ... The organizations that benefited most were the ones that had reorganized their governance around human bits and machine bits.

### Representation gap (9:12)
> In traditional technology workforce, 36% of technical talent is female. In our evolving AI workforce, it's 31%. That's not the right direction.
